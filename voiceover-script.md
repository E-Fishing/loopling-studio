# Woofer voiceover script

Record one audio file per line and name it exactly like the code in front of it, for example `L1-S2-1.m4a`.
Any common format works: .m4a (phone voice memos), .mp3 or .wav. Leave a short pause before and after each line.
Lines marked _(after the task)_ play right after the learner finishes that step.

There are **143 clips** in total.

## Lesson 1: Welcome to the studio

**Step 1**

- `L1-S1-1` Hi, I’m Woofer! I’m a little speaker, so I love music. I’ll show you how music software works, one small step at a time.
- `L1-S1-2` Apps like FL Studio, GarageBand and Ableton are called DAWs (Digital Audio Workstations). That just means a music studio inside your computer.
- `L1-S1-3` These are your tracks. Each track is like one musician in a band: drums, bass, chords and melody.
- `L1-S1-4` This big area is the editor. It’s where you write music for the track you picked.
- `L1-S1-5` Up here are the transport controls. They work like the buttons on a music player.

**Step 2** (task: Press Play)

- `L1-S2-1` I loaded a short demo song so you can hear what we’ll build together.
- `L1-S2-2` Press the green Play button. The Spacebar works too!
- `L1-S2-done` Sounds good, right? Watch my speaker cone. It pumps along with the music!  _(after the task)_

**Step 3**

- `L1-S3-1` See the highlight sliding across the grid? That’s the playhead. It marks the moment you’re hearing.
- `L1-S3-2` Music is counted in bars and beats: 1, 2, 3, 4, then a new bar starts. This box keeps count for you.

**Step 4** (task: Change the tempo)

- `L1-S4-1` The tempo is the speed of a song, measured in BPM: beats per minute.
- `L1-S4-2` Press + or − to change it, and listen to the difference.
- `L1-S4-done` Slow songs sit around 70 to 90 BPM. Dance music is usually 120 to 130.  _(after the task)_

**Step 5** (task: Turn on the click)

- `L1-S5-1` Now turn on the Click. It’s a metronome: a tick on every beat.
- `L1-S5-done` Count along with it: 1, 2, 3, 4! The high tick is beat 1.  _(after the task)_

**Step 6** (task: Stop playback)

- `L1-S6-1` Nice counting! Now press Stop. Turn the click off too, if you like.

**Step 7**

- `L1-S7-1` One more tip: point at anything, and the hint bar explains what it does.
- `L1-S7-2` Faded buttons are locked for now. They unlock as you learn them, so nothing gets overwhelming.
- `L1-S7-3` Next, you’ll make your very own beat. Ready?

- `L1-complete` Lesson 1 complete! You can play and stop a song, read bars and beats, and change the tempo. Up next: Your first beat.  _(end of lesson)_

**Step 3** (task: Kick on steps 1, 5, 9 and 13)

- `L2-S3-1` Each box is a moment in time. Songs have 4 beats in each bar, and each beat splits into 4 steps.
- `L2-S3-2` The bold numbers 1, 5, 9 and 13 are where each beat starts.

**Step 4** (task: Press Play)

- `L2-S4-1` Press Play to hear your kick drum!

- `L2-S5-1` Now the Snare. Put it on steps 5 and 13.
- `L2-S5-2` Those are beats 2 and 4, the backbeat. Almost every pop, rock and hip-hop song has one.

**Step 6** (task: Add 6 Closed Hats)

- `L2-S6-1` Hi-hats fill the gaps between the big hits.
- `L2-S6-2` Add a Closed Hat on every other step: 1, 3, 5, 7… Tip: click and drag to paint lots at once!

**Step 7**

- `L2-S7-2` There are no wrong answers. If you don’t like a change, Undo takes you back.

- `L2-complete` Lesson 2 complete! You know how bars, beats and steps work, and you built a beat in a step sequencer. Up next: Notes and the piano roll.  _(end of lesson)_

## Lesson 5: Music theory: why chords work

**Step 1** (task: Click the Chords track)

- `L5-S1-1` You’ve been using chords. Now let’s look inside them and find out why they sound good!
- `L5-S1-2` Click the Chords track.

**Step 2** (task: Play every note of the scale)

- `L5-S2-1` Every key is built from a scale: a set of notes that sound good together. Your song’s key and scale are shown here.
- `L5-S2-2` On this piano, the bright keys are the notes of your scale. The greyed keys are outside it.
- `L5-S2-3` Play every note of the scale: the bright keys, one after another. Your computer keyboard works too.
- `L5-S2-done` That’s the scale your whole song is built from.  _(after the task)_

**Step 3** (task: Play a triad)

- `L5-S3-1` A chord is built from the scale too. Pick a note, skip the next one, take one, skip one, take one.
- `L5-S3-2` Those 3 notes make a triad, the most common kind of chord. Its bottom note is called the root.
- `L5-S3-3` Play a triad: hold Q, E and T together, or click those three piano keys one after another.
- `L5-S3-done` Root, skip one, third, skip one, fifth. Every triad is built that way!  _(after the task)_

**Step 4** (task: Play a major and a minor chord)

- `L5-S4-1` Chords come in two main flavors. Major sounds bright and happy. Minor sounds sad or dreamy.
- `L5-S4-2` The only difference is the middle note. Move it down by one key, and a major chord turns minor.
- `L5-S4-3` Play a major chord, then a minor one. Try Q E T, then Q 3 T.
- `L5-S4-done` Hear the mood change? Just one note made the difference.  _(after the task)_

**Step 5** (task: Hear chords 1, 4 and 5)

- `L5-S5-1` Build a triad on each note of the scale, and you get the chords of your key. These buttons are those chords.
- `L5-S5-2` Musicians number them in Roman numerals: I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi, vii°. Capital letters are major chords, small letters are minor.
- `L5-S5-3` Click chords 1, 4 and 5. They’re the most used chords in all of music.

**Step 6** (task: Play chord 5, then chord 1)

- `L5-S6-1` Chord 5 (V) sounds like a question. Chord 1 (I) sounds like the answer: home.
- `L5-S6-2` Going from V to I is called a cadence. It’s how songs feel finished.
- `L5-S6-3` Click chord 5, then chord 1, and hear it land home.
- `L5-S6-done` Home sweet home! That pull from V to I is in almost every song you know.  _(after the task)_

**Step 7**

- `L5-S7-1` Now you know why famous progressions work. The pop hit progression is just I, V, vi and IV.
- `L5-S7-2` Next time you hear a song, listen for the chord that sounds like home!

- `L5-complete` Lesson 5 complete! You know what scales and triads are, how major and minor chords differ, how chords get their numbers, and why V wants to go home to I. Up next: Melody and recording.  _(end of lesson)_

- `L7-S5-1` Pan moves a sound left or right in your headphones.
- `L7-S5-2` Slide the Melody’s pan a little to one side.

**Step 6** (task: Add reverb to the Chords)

- `L7-S6-1` Effects change how a sound feels. Reverb makes it sound like a big room.
- `L7-S6-2` Turn up the reverb on the Chords.

- `L7-S7-1` Keep an eye on this meter. If it turns red, the song is too loud and may crackle.
- `L7-S7-2` If that happens, lower a few faders instead.
